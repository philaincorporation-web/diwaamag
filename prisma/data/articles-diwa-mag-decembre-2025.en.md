=== slug: gabon-defie-fatalite-economique-strategie-numerique-foret
titre: Gabon defies economic fatalism: towards a revolutionary digital strategy rooted in the forest
chapeau: Digital Economy Minister Mark Alexandre Doumba wants digital technology to create jobs for young people and to unlock the value of a forest capital estimated at 105 billion dollars.
---
Libreville, Gabon – **Digital Economy Minister Mark Alexandre DOUMBA** recently set out his vision for Gabon's future, promising a radical transformation of the digital sector. With youth unemployment considered alarming, reaching 20 to 30% in emerging countries, the stated goal is to make digital technology a "creator of new jobs" and a "genuine source of hope, opportunity and dignity" for Gabonese youth.

## "The climate, a 105-billion-dollar economic engine"

The digital ambition lies at the heart of the country's ecological heritage. The Minister stressed an often underestimated reality: 92% of Gabon's territory is covered by primary forest and, as a result, "the climate is an economy".

Citing data from a World Bank study, the government highlighted the country's untapped potential: **the national economy "would be estimated at 105 billion dollars"** if the value of its forests were fully taken into account, five times the current GDP. The digital strategy and digitalisation are therefore seen as the key tool for monetising and protecting this "unique climate asset".

## Overcoming slowness: automation as a "remedy"

To make this vision a reality, speed is essential. The Minister expressed his "concern and determination" that the digital economy "should accelerate as much in public administration as in the private sector".

During a dialogue with the ecosystem in June 2025, stakeholders identified the main "structural barriers": lack of funding, administrative red tape and inadequate infrastructure.

Faced with these obstacles, the administration is being called upon to modernise. The Minister made it clear that it operates at an "insufficient pace" and that "automation is the remedy" needed to boost productivity.

In addition, to consolidate Gabon's position as a "key African player in digital transformation", legislative reforms are essential. The dialogue with the ecosystem highlighted an "inadequate legal framework" that must be brought into line with international standards, particularly in terms of cybersecurity (the theme of the 3rd edition of the DigieWomen Awards 2025) and data protection.

## The funding challenge: accepting the initial risk

Financing innovation is recognised as a real issue. The Minister did not shy away from the "harsh reality of digital entrepreneurship", stating that a start-up unfortunately has to "lose money before it makes money" and that "financing innovation is difficult everywhere in the world". To attract investors able to take on this initial risk, the government is considering concrete solutions. Public funding mechanisms or specific tax incentives are planned for tech companies and start-ups willing to "question themselves, pivot and persevere".

As for infrastructure, whose shortcomings are an obstacle cited by those on the ground, the Ministry held strategic meetings with operator executives (Moov Africa, Airtel Gabon, Vivendi Africa). Immediate actions resulting from these discussions are expected to improve the quality and accessibility of services.

## A new collaborative and inclusive approach

The Ministry has promised an "approach to developing the digital economy that differs from what has been done before in this country". This roadmap will be drawn up through collaborative thematic committees, whose work will be turned into "concrete initiatives by the government".

Inclusion is a fundamental pillar of this strategy, with a strong commitment to ensuring the "sustainability of women's entrepreneurial initiatives in digital" and the "effective promotion of local talent". The DigieWomen Awards initiative is of "strategic importance", since its mission is to "map and identify women digital leaders in Africa". The identification of these "leading heroines" (such as the Digital Businesswoman of the Year or the Woman in Artificial Intelligence) is used by the Ministry to encourage the new generation and female students to get involved in digital.

Gabon, determined to consolidate its place as a key African player, is embarking on a process of accelerated modernisation, in which protecting its forest capital goes hand in hand with technological ambition.

---
Gabon is moving rapidly into the digital age. With the **"Digital Gabon"** programme, officially launched on **5 November 2024**, the government is undertaking an unprecedented transformation of its administration. The goal: to make digital technology the lever of more efficient, transparent governance that serves all citizens.

Supported by the **World Bank** to the tune of **44 billion CFA francs**, the project marks a new stage in the national strategy to modernise the State. It is built around **13 flagship projects**, including the introduction of the **Personal Identification Number (NIP)**, the digitisation of administrative procedures, the single social register and the e-Procurement platform for managing public contracts.

## Strong commitment and concrete achievements

In one year, the Gabonese government has laid solid foundations for a more connected and efficient administration. Notable progress includes the adoption in September 2025 of a comprehensive legal framework to govern the digitisation of public services, guarantee digital sovereignty and strengthen the transparency of government action.

This legal framework supports the overall **"Digital Gabon"** strategy, which aims to provide universal access to digital technologies and step up the fight against corruption.

On the ground, digital infrastructure is developing rapidly: fibre optics are being extended across the country, **sovereign data centres are being built and cybersecurity is being reinforced**. These investments, made in partnership with the World Bank, form the essential technical foundation for transforming the Gabonese administration.

## Digital inclusion at the heart of the "Digital Gabon" project

The digital revolution is not limited to the State: it extends to the whole population. As part of this drive, the government launched the "1 student, 1 computer" programme, a strong symbol of a proactive digital inclusion policy. The aim is simple and ambitious: to give Gabonese youth real access to the tools and skills of the 21st century.

At the same time, the country is consolidating strategic partnerships with major international digital players. Thanks to its collaboration with Visa, Gabon is modernising tax collection, accelerating the digitisation of payments and strengthening financial inclusion through the distribution of virtual bank cards. The government is also working with Huawei to speed up the digitisation of public services and promote the use of technology within the administration.

## Digital tools for more effective governance

Modernisation also translates into concrete administrative innovations: the introduction of electronic signatures, the roll-out of electronic document management (EDM) software, and the deployment of integrated platforms to improve the management of human resources and public companies. These tools reduce paperwork, streamline communication between institutions and make decision-making processes more transparent.

## A lever for sovereignty and development

Beyond technology, Digital Gabon is a national sovereignty project. By relying on local skills and control of its infrastructure, the country intends to secure its strategic data and strengthen its digital autonomy. This vision is part of a regional ambition: to make Gabon a digital hub in Central Africa. With modernised infrastructure, a robust legal framework and targeted international partnerships, the country is now positioning itself as a major player in digital transformation on the continent.

## How would you describe the importance of Big Data in Gabon's current professional context?

Gabon is at an important digital turning point. Companies accumulate data, but very few really make use of it. Big Data makes it possible to improve governance, optimise costs, automate complex tasks, increase transparency and make decisions based on facts rather than intuition.

## What advice would you give to a beginner who wants to get serious about data science?

For Data Analysis, you need a practical, business-oriented approach. Excel (data cleaning, pivot tables, charts), Power BI or Tableau, essential statistics (mean, median, variance, correlation), and data storytelling. Mindset: rigour and analytical sense.

For Data Science, you need essential foundations: mathematics (statistics, probability, linear algebra), Python and SQL, and an understanding of algorithms (regression, classification, clustering). Mindset: experimentation, curiosity, documentation, deep understanding.

## DIWA MAG: Many citizens wonder whether they can complain when they have problems with their operators. Is this possible, and if so, how?

Every citizen, as a consumer of electronic communications services, has the full right to make their voice heard. They can send complaints directly to the operators concerned. And if no satisfactory solution is provided, they are fully entitled to refer the matter to the regulator, in this case ARCEP, for redress. This mechanism ensures that users' rights are respected and that operators remain attentive to the public's concerns.

If consumers are not satisfied by the operator, they can then contact ARCEP through the following channels: **website, call centre, post, email**.

As soon as ARCEP receives a complaint with supporting documents, a well-defined procedure is triggered to ensure fair handling. It takes place in several stages:

- The complaint is forwarded to the competent department for investigation;
- An acknowledgement of receipt is sent to the complainant, confirming that their request has been registered;
- The operator concerned is informed and invited to submit its observations;
- A thorough analysis of the complaint and the documents provided is carried out;
- Finally, a reasoned decision is issued and communicated to the parties concerned.

This process aims to protect consumers' rights while encouraging constructive dialogue between stakeholders in the sector.

## DIWA MAG: In a world shaped by the rise of artificial intelligence and cybersecurity, how is ARCEP adapting its regulation?

As early as 2001, Law No. 005/2001 of 1 June 2001 regulating telecommunications in the Gabonese Republic defined the Electronic Communications and Postal Regulatory Authority (ARCEP) as an independent administrative authority.

Cybersecurity and its corollary, cybercrime, are covered by Law No. 027/2023 of 12 July 2023 regulating cybersecurity and the fight against cybercrime in the Gabonese Republic, which aims to strengthen critical infrastructure, protect sensitive data and punish online abuse, such as insults and threats on social media.

Artificial Intelligence (AI) is referred to in Law No. 025/2023 of 12 July 2023 amending Law No. 001/2011 of 25 September 2011 on the protection of personal data, where it is defined as a logical and automated process generally based on an algorithm capable of performing well-defined tasks.

With the rapid development of electronic communications networks and services, and their socio-economic consequences, Law No. 026/2018 regulating electronic communications in the Gabonese Republic set new general objectives, including: protecting users of electronic communications networks and services; and promoting innovation, investment and employment in the electronic communications sector.

Aware of the growing challenges of digital transformation, ARCEP is committed to building a clear, stable and coherent regulatory framework. This work is carried out in close collaboration with other national bodies such as the Authority for the Protection of Personal Data and Privacy (APDPVP) and the High Authority for Communication (HAC). Together, we work in a spirit of co-regulation to guarantee users a trusted digital environment, in line with the objectives set by Law No. 26/2018.

But these challenges go beyond national borders, because regulating Artificial Intelligence, Cybersecurity and converging technologies requires international coordination. That is why Gabon actively takes part in the work of the International Telecommunication Union (ITU), notably within the World Summit on the Information Society (WSIS).

At the 2025 edition, action line C10, devoted to ethics in AI and converging technologies, was highlighted, underlining the importance of shared standards for an ethical and inclusive digital world. This session presented the results of UNESCO's work, highlighting forward-looking analyses of the recommendation on the ethics of neurotechnology, at a time when AI, quantum, neurotechnology, biotechnology and other cutting-edge fields are entering a phase of convergence.

Faced with such complex issues, it is clear that no country can act alone. Only strong, concerted international cooperation can establish effective regulatory frameworks. The objective is clear: to ensure that these technological innovations serve the common good, while respecting the fundamental principles of human dignity, social inclusion and environmental sustainability.
